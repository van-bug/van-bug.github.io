**Bio:**

Dr. Tuan Amith is an assistant professor with School of Health Information Sciences from the University of Victoria. He received his PhD in Biomedical Informatics from the McWilliams School of Biomedical Informatics at the University of Texas Health Science Center at Houston (UTHealth), under the supervision of Dr. Cui Tao. 

He also has BS and MS in Computer Science and Software Engineering from the University of Houston. He previously served as faculty at UTHealth and University of Texas Medical Branch. His primary research focus is on the application of semantic web technologies and biomedical ontologies for clinical and public health subjects. 

His past research has received best paper distinctions AMIA, MedInfo, and IEEE, and his dissertation was a finalist for the American Medical Informatics Association for best dissertation. He has served as PI and Co-I on past and current NIH-funded research. In addition, Dr. Amith is a member of the editorial board for Nature's Scientific Reports (Digital Health), BMC Medical Informatics and Decision Making, and the Journal of Clinical Informatics.

**Abstract:**

Distributed computing systems offer some advanced benefits to share and manage data and information. Theoretically, if these systems are coupled with semantics they can further improve and promote the aforementioned benefits for archiving data and information for bioinformatics. 

In this talk, I will discuss two exploratory research ventures for vaccine informatics. One vision involves the development and the application of semantic blockchains to manage human papillomavirus (HPV) vaccine immunization information between clinics. The other venture explores the potential of using the nanopublication network to archive and distribute vaccine information from the Vaccine Investigation and Online Information Network (VIOLIN). 

I will share initial findings and outputs with these two projects and lay out our future direction to continue this work.
